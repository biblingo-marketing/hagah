## Who it is for

Primary persona (D1 desk research, high confidence on the persona, moderate on size): an adult evangelical layperson or pastor, Reformed/Baptist skew, following Andy Davis's "extended memorization" method or a variant. Practices with printed sheets, index cards and self-recorded audio, fitted into commutes, showers, dishes, treadmills and bedtime. Says "Scripture memory," "big chunks," "whole books," "word for word." Does not say "spaced repetition" or "verbatim." Does not know the word "hagah."

Secondary: seminary-trained Greek keepers, who are a real but tiny and unserved group — no visible community memorizes Greek text today. Hagah's Greek track is category creation, not a wedge.

## The job

Memorize a whole book of the Bible, word for word, and still have it a year later — using the hours that already exist in the day rather than hours that do not.

## The three block types

| Block | I/O | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screen | Eyes and hands free, phone available | First exposure and encoding. New material is introduced only here. |
| Audio | Hands-free, eyes on the road | Anticipate-then-check review. No screen interaction required. |
| Recall | No device at all | Silent free recall. Read-only card beforehand, grading afterward. |

## Non-goals

A verse-a-day habit. Streaks, badges, leaderboards, church group pages. A comprehension course. A Bible-study suite. An AI companion. General memory training — the evidence says there is no far transfer and copy must not imply otherwise (SAFE-2).

## UX principles

- Glanceable at arm's length in a moving car: large targets, one decision per screen.
- No scroll during encode.
- Every audio-first state has a visual mirror, and every visual state a non-visual path.
- Missed days re-plan silently. No debt counter, no scolding, nothing that manufactures streak anxiety.
- The app never shows a scheduling number. Stability is a color.
