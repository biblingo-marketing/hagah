# Verbatim Scripture Memorization — Evidence Review (summary)

Context: Nick's long-term project to memorize whole Bible books verbatim (Romans, Sermon on the Mount; English and Koine Greek). Current practice: a 15-minute morning encoding slot with a screen, silent subvocal free recall on a 30–40 minute run six days a week, and audio-cued recall on two 40-minute commutes on three office days.

The ten ranked changes below are the source of most invariants in `docs/engine/protocol.md`. Each protocol invariant carries its own evidence tier and confidence; this file exists so the reasoning is traceable.

## Top-ranked changes

1. **Add an accuracy backbone.** Anticipate-then-check on commutes, not simultaneous shadowing. A weekly aloud or typed check against the text. A listener where possible. The run is retrieval practice, never verification.
2. **First-day protocol.** One aloud read → overt recall with immediate re-read feedback → three clean recitations → stop. No read-once-then-test without feedback; no in-session overlearning.
3. **Fix chunk boundaries at discourse units and never move them.** A compound cue per chunk start (number + Greek opening particle + route landmark), and one "why this follows" line per seam.
4. **Schedule at the pericope in FSRS** (cue = preceding clause). Whole-book run-throughs are a separate expanding integrity schedule: 2 weeks → 4 weeks → 6–8 weeks → quarterly.
5. **Three-tier day** (sabaq / sabqi / manzil) with criterion-based graduation. Watch for the new-material overload cliff.
6. **Test before adding a new Pauline letter**, and build a divergence table for shared formulae.
7. **Daily five-minute seams-only pass**; krama-style overlap drilling on failing seams.
8. **Argument map and random-entry drill before the chain.** Spoken verse numbers as fine-grained addresses.
9. **Run order:** consolidated material first (~20 min), fragile material after. Keep the run positioned after encoding.
10. **Two to three minutes of evening re-recitation** of the morning unit, with first retrieval the next morning.

## The framing finding

Verbatim recall is **regenerated from meaning plus an activated lexical and syntactic set** (Potter & Lombardi 1990/1998), not replayed from a stored string. So errors are fluent synonym and connective swaps and wrong-paragraph starts, and they cluster at seams.

This is why the diff engine classifies error types rather than merely counting them (DIFF-1), why seams get their own cards (SCH-2), and why a fluent paraphrase must score as a miss rather than a pass (DIFF-3).

## Open assumption

That Greek and English of a passage are learned concurrently rather than sequentially. No study exists on memorizing the same long text verbatim in two languages. Shipped as an instrumented experiment (E2 in the protocol), defaulting new users to English only.
